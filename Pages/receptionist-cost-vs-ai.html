How much does a receptionist cost vs AI?

Answering the phone is essential, but the way you do it can cost anywhere from $15 to $4,000+ a month. Here is what each option really costs a small business.

Updated October 2026 · By the Vocallus team

Option 1: an in-house receptionist

In the US, receptionists earned a median of $18.27 an hour, about $38,010 a year, in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. That is pay only. Payroll taxes, benefits, training and time off add more on top.

A full-time receptionist also covers about 40 hours of a 168-hour week. Calls at lunch, after hours, on weekends and while they are on another line still go to voicemail.

Option 2: a live answering service

Services with human receptionists usually charge by the minute or by the call. Public starting prices in 2026 include about $250/month for 50 receptionist minutes (Ruby) and about $292.50/month for 30 calls (Smith.ai's human plans). A busy month can cost several hundred to a few thousand dollars.

Option 3: an AI receptionist

AI receptionists answer every call instantly, day and night, and can book appointments and take messages. Prices range from about $15 to a few hundred dollars a month. Vocallus starts at $14.99/month.

Which is right for you?

  • Mostly booking and simple questions? An AI receptionist covers it for the least money.
  • Need a person in the office anyway? Keep your receptionist and use AI for lunch, after hours and overflow.
  • Complex, sensitive calls all day? A human receptionist or live service is worth the cost.

Many businesses do both: staff answer during the day, and an AI receptionist picks up everything they miss.

Questions

How much does a receptionist cost per year?+

The median US receptionist pay was about $38,010 a year in May 2025 (BLS), before taxes and benefits.

Is an AI receptionist cheaper than an answering service?+

Usually, yes. AI receptionists start around $15/month, while live answering services usually start around $250/month.

Can an AI receptionist replace a front desk?+

For answering, booking and messages, often yes. For in-person tasks like checking people in, you still need a person.
